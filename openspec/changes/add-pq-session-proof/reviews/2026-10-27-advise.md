## Blind take

The following ten lines were persisted in `2026-10-27-blind-take.md` after reading the proposal, living spec, and cited code, before opening the delta, `design.md`, or `tasks.md`. They are questions the contract must answer, not a proposed replacement design.

1. Pin the opening to the verifier's accepted current head, checking both its digest and XID; supplied keys cannot confer authority.
2. Refuse an opening when authority is absent or forked, even if a detached signature checks under a remembered key.
3. Pin whether an established session survives rotation or a detected fork; current-authority proofs must recheck current trust.
4. Pin canonical, versioned, domain-separated opening and MAC transcripts with unambiguous field encodings.
5. Bind the opening to the intended audience and verifier-issued fresh challenge, with explicit ownership of challenge consumption.
6. Bind each MAC to the accepted opening, XID, digest, audience, and actual proved message; resist cross-session substitution.
7. Pin follow-up replay semantics and who owns freshness state; a valid MAC alone does not demonstrate a new action.
8. Refuse a claim of authenticated session establishment unless the shared secret is bound to the signed opening; leaving establishment out of scope must be explicit at the API boundary.
9. Pin failure behavior so rejected inputs neither consume valid sessions nor advance replay state, and require constant-time MAC verification.
10. Tradeoff: symmetric follow-ups save signature cost but every session-key holder can forge them, so they cannot provide transferable attribution or a public proof log.

## Comparison and steelman

The opening correctly uses `Trust::resolve`, which accepts only the current head's digest (`state.rs:348`), and inherits the fork refusal in `authority` (`state.rs:338`). The delta explicitly requires the head for the named XID. Classical openers are excluded. The signature has a versioned domain and an empty ML-DSA context consistent with the state-chain convention. Audience/digest mismatch rejection, no second signature, no proof log, and the ability of every key holder to produce proofs are all answered.

The strongest reading of the author's scope is a small verification primitive with caller-managed trust, freshness, and secret material. That is a reasonable boundary. It does not need a transport, a replay database, a new resolver, or an ML-KEM implementation. The state digest already commits the XID, so a MAC need not duplicate the XID if the verifier binds and checks the session context. Follow-up MACs may intentionally be reusable statements. A session may intentionally retain authority from its opening rather than assert current authority on every message. These interpretations answer several concerns in my blind take without requiring its particular mechanisms.

However, none of those caller preconditions or lifetime semantics is currently specified. The gaps below permit materially different security behavior while satisfying the existing tasks. They require contract decisions before implementation, not an expansion into key establishment.

## Findings requiring amendment

### R1 — Freshness has no verifier contract

`specs/session-proof/spec.md:8–10` requires a fresh challenge, but the positive opening scenario at lines 14–18 requires only a verifying signature. `design.md:8–18` supplies trust and an opening without identifying an expected challenge or audience supplied independently by the verifier. A captured signed opening can therefore satisfy the described checks again; merely signing a 32-byte value does not establish freshness.

Pin who issues, retains, compares, and consumes the challenge, and how the expected audience is supplied. Caller-owned state is sufficient if it is an explicit precondition with a defined check/consume boundary. Add rejection scenarios for an unexpected challenge, a consumed challenge, and an audience different from the verifier's expectation. Specify whether follow-up replay is permitted; reusable MAC statements are acceptable if the contract does not present them as fresh actions. No persistent proof log is needed.

### R2 — The supplied secret has no binding to the accepted opening

`design.md:22–24,31–33` introduces a caller-held key after opening, while the signature contains no key commitment and the MAC contains no opening identifier or challenge. The contract never requires that the supplied key belong to that authenticated opening. If two openings for the same audience and digest use the same supplied key, a MAC from one verifies in the other under the stated transcript. More seriously, a signed opening by itself cannot authenticate an independently attached secret chosen by another party.

Keep key establishment out of scope. Specify the verification API's precondition that external establishment binds the secret to the authenticated opening and peer, and how the accepted session retains that association. Pin the mechanism or precondition that prevents cross-opening MAC reuse (for example an opening-bound MAC context or a guaranteed per-opening key binding). Do not silently assume the unverified ML-KEM hypothesis provides it. Include a scenario for two distinct openings sharing the same audience/digest, and distinguish successful signature checking from possession of an authenticated session secret. Binding a MAC to an opening alone is not a substitute for authenticating the secret.

### R3 — Authority after opening is unspecified

`specs/session-proof/spec.md:5–7` checks current authority at opening. Follow-up verification at lines 28–39 checks only the saved session context. The scenario called “An older digest fails” at lines 47–51 actually tests a digest different from the opening; it does not test a session opened at H after the verifier advances to H2. Likewise, a subsequently detected fork removes state-chain authority but has no specified effect on that session.

Choose and state whether follow-ups assert current authority or authority captured at opening. For current authority, require resolution against current verifier trust on each check and rejection after rotation, loss of authority, or a fork. For captured authority, explicitly define that lifetime/revocation boundary and explain that successful MACs do not assert current state-chain authority. Either choice needs scenarios for rotation and a fork after a successful opening. This is an ambiguity, not evidence that the living state-chain spec already mandates one session-lifetime policy.

### R4 — The cryptographic transcripts are field lists, not an interoperable encoding

`design.md:14–18,22–24` and the delta list signed/MACed fields but do not define their byte encoding, framing, or the MAC domain. Two implementations can choose incompatible representations, or an unframed concatenation can admit different field boundaries. The state-chain implementation already provides a canonical encoding convention, but the session contract does not adopt one.

Pin exact versioned opening and MAC encodings, including field types, lengths, unambiguous boundaries, and distinct domain separation. Define strict decoding/rejection and full tag length; use constant-time tag comparison. Include a byte-level vector and malformed/ambiguous-encoding checks. This is a small wire-contract decision and can reuse the existing canonical dCBOR approach.

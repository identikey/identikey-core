## Blind take

These ten lines were written in the reader's commentary after reading only the proposal's Why, the living `pq-state-chain` spec, and cited code, before opening the delta, design, tasks, or previous review. They identify concerns the author must answer, not a replacement design.

1. Pin the opening to the XID and the digest of the verifier's current accepted head.
2. Resolve authority locally; an opening must not supply its own trusted control key.
3. Bind the opening to the intended verifier and a challenge that verifier already holds.
4. Define when successful verification consumes the challenge and what failed attempts preserve.
5. Refuse to treat an ML-DSA signature as secret session-key establishment.
6. Bind each MAC to one authenticated opening so shared key material cannot mix sessions.
7. Refuse cached session authority after the cited head rotates or becomes forked.
8. Pin canonical signed and MACed bytes, distinct domains, tag length, and comparison behavior.
9. State whether repeated valid MACs remain acceptable; authentication alone does not provide replay prevention.
10. Tradeoff: symmetric follow-ups save signature cost but let every session-key holder forge them, so they cannot establish individual authorship.

## Comparison and steelman

The amended contract answers these concerns while keeping a useful, bounded verifier API. The strongest case for the author's boundary is that trust, challenge state, and externally authenticated key material can be caller-owned. The verifier need not implement transport, a proof log, or key establishment to specify checks over those inputs. Challenge consumption belongs to successful opening verification; supplying the externally bound key is the additional condition for a session. This permits staged verification without claiming that the signature establishes a secret.

My blind preference for current authority is not itself a reason to reject a contract that deliberately chose captured authority. Here the author explicitly chooses current authority on every follow-up, which agrees with the living resolver boundary. Likewise, reusable MAC statements are a valid choice: the contract expressly refuses to interpret them as fresh actions. The MAC need not duplicate the XID because its opening id commits the unsigned opening, including the XID, and the retained session binds that context. Requiring a new exchange or extra wire fields would expand this review beyond the unresolved concerns.

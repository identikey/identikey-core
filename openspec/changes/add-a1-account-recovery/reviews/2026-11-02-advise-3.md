## Blind take

1. Pin account recovery to access restoration; it must never yield key material or signing authority.
2. Replace the public `granted()` bypass with proof issuance that requires a verified factor.
3. Bind every attempt to its purpose, channel, destination, and intended account.
4. Refuse caller-selected recovery targets that lack an authoritative destination binding.
5. Define how destination removal or reassignment invalidates outstanding recovery authority.
6. Check current custody when granting access; historical C1/C2 eligibility is insufficient.
7. Make factor redemption and proof acceptance single-use, including concurrent calls.
8. Bound attempts and proofs by expiry, with explicit terminal failure behavior.
9. Keep recovery enrollment on the same XID and preserve ordinary passkey semantics.
10. Tradeoff: revalidation supports revocation but adds state reads and requires a clear acceptance boundary.

## Comparison and steelman

The author answers the concerns with a library-owned immutable binding, trusted directory reads, committed factor transitions, and consuming acceptance. The existing code's public `granted()` and `Clone + Copy` proof (`crates/identikey-recovery/src/lib.rs:76`) are implementation work expressly covered by R3; they do not invalidate this pre-implementation contract review. Living managed-custody requirements at `openspec/specs/managed-custody/spec.md:915` preserve ordinary replacement passkeys and forbid recovery-session signing or key access. The amendment preserves those boundaries.

The strongest alternative to my revalidation concern is that finish could intentionally freeze destination authority and issue a transferable-in-time capability. The previous review allowed that policy if explicit. The author instead chooses revocation at acceptance: a successful finish does not establish permanent authority over the destination. That is coherent and resolves the ambiguity without requiring a different ceremony.

My blind take's proof-expiry preference is not a new gate. The accepted R3 contract bounds use through consuming acceptance and current authorization checks; a separate proof TTL was explicitly not required by the previous review. Likewise, this review does not require a transaction spanning directory lookup and an arbitrary caller closure. The specified boundary is the acceptance recheck before invoking trusted integration code, not a guarantee that the directory cannot change during callback execution. Production port wiring remains a later route change.

## R2 — Answered: revalidate at acceptance and consume on refusal

`design.md:88` through the Binding section and delta `specs/account-recovery/spec.md:142` retain the trusted channel/destination/purpose/XID binding. The callback receives the original bound destination and XID and must use them. A directory reassignment therefore cannot retarget the existing proof from A to B.

The new rule at `design.md:94` and delta `specs/account-recovery/spec.md:149` expressly requires `accept_recover` to consult the directory again. An unbound destination or a binding to another XID refuses acceptance and consumes the proof. Current-custody eligibility remains an additional check, as required by `design.md:58` and delta `specs/account-recovery/spec.md:73`; retaining D-to-A binding cannot authorize a now-C3/C4 account.

The two new scenarios at delta `specs/account-recovery/spec.md:160` and `:166` start with a finished recover proof for D and A while A remains C2. Removal and reassignment to B each produce zero callback invocations and a consumed proof. Keeping A at C2 isolates destination invalidation from custody rejection. The immutable binding rule supplies the bound target, and the single-use rule at delta `specs/account-recovery/spec.md:180` prevents retrying the refused proof even if the destination later returns to A. This is deliberately different from refusal before finish, which leaves the factor unconsumed (`design.md:92`). The lifecycle stages are now unambiguous.

The two R2 follow-up commitments in `tasks.md:31` and `:32` cover the policy and implementation-test obligation, including callback count, bound target, and proof disposition. Their checked state records contract reconciliation; it does not claim those tests already exist. No new gap or owed checkbox is warranted.

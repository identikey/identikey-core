## Blind independent take

1. Pin a versioned, deterministic record encoding that binds the XID, sequence, predecessor, control key, and operational keys under a domain-separated signature.
2. Pin migration as a one-time transition from classical authority into PQ authority for each verifier and XID.
3. Refuse a later classical Elect signature or replacement checkpoint as a way to reset established PQ control.
4. Distinguish the retained bootstrap digest from the advancing accepted head; replay of the anchor cannot roll back the head.
5. Pin predecessor-authorized transitions and possession of the replacement key, with no unsigned authority-bearing fields.
6. Refuse two valid divergent successors without returning either authority, including conflicts discovered after accepting one branch.
7. Pin durable verifier state sufficient to preserve head monotonicity and known duplicity across calls.
8. Keep XID derivation stable and specify the concrete PQ profile and rejection of unsupported versions or suites.
9. Keep caller-supplied chain verification separate from the unchanged well-known document body and the existing URN grant.
10. Accept the tradeoff that local anchoring and fail-closed conflict detection do not prove freshness or reveal a branch never supplied to the verifier.

## Comparison and steelman

**R1 is answered.** The delta at lines 21–23 makes trust state local to the verifier and keyed by XID, forbids resolver bytes or a server-named digest from establishing that state, and restricts classical migration to an XID with no head. It explicitly forbids both anchor replay moving an existing head and a different first record replacing an established chain. Lines 37–47 exercise those two bypasses; line 23 also refuses acceptance after loss of migration trust state once classical signatures are outside policy. The design at lines 15–23 confirms that both inception entrances are initialization only. Bootstrap authorization is therefore no longer a second rotation path.

**R2 is answered.** Delta lines 87–105 require no authority once both valid siblings have been supplied, regardless of arrival order, and exclude identical repeats and invalid signatures from duplicity. Design line 35 says “Once both have been supplied,” so this cannot reasonably be implemented as merely checking candidates against the newest head and discarding a late sibling as stale. For H → A followed by valid H → B, the result is no authority, not A or B; a later lookup of A's digest cannot bypass that result. An implementation must retain or reconstruct enough authenticated ancestry and conflict evidence to honor that obligation. “At most one anchor digest and one head” limits accepted authority, not the evidence needed to verify it. This matches the living xid-resolution no-winner requirement without claiming that its existing provenance comparator already handles PQ records.

**R3 is answered by the explicitly permitted scope reduction.** Delta lines 107–115 define verification of caller-supplied records, preserve the stored XID-document response body, add no resolution tier, and limit authority references to the accepted head. Resolver carriage of the chain is named as follow-up work outside this change. The proposal's journey agrees. There is no longer a promise to deliver these bytes through an undefined well-known representation.

**The v1 and signing-context amendment is answered.** Delta line 5 commits the authority-bearing fields in canonical dCBOR, fixes exactly one ML-DSA-65 control key and threshold 1, rejects other profiles and versions, hashes the unsigned encoding, and requires signatures over a protocol-and-version domain followed by the record. Design lines 5–7 include `v` and define first sequence/absent predecessor. Exact map encodings and context bytes still belong in the codec implementation and vectors before interoperability is claimed; the first task now explicitly owns that work. This is sufficient for the architecture contract being reviewed.

**Rotation and authentication boundaries remain sound within this scope.** Delta lines 61–83 require the previous control authority and possession of newly introduced keys; classical document updates cannot move the PQ head. The living URN requirement remains unchanged (`openspec/specs/oidc-provider/spec.md:242`). The current `crates/identikey-oidc/src/provider.rs:634` calls `verify_response` and resolves the subject from its classical public key. The sibling protocol's `crates/identikey-auth/src/verify.rs:57–82` likewise returns a classical fingerprint after checking the optional PQ signature. Design line 41 correctly makes no claim that adding state upgrades that authentication flow.

The strongest objection from my independent take is the absence of a storage schema or separate persistent fork flag. I do not require either as a competing design: caller-owned state and authenticated history can meet the stated observable behavior. Known conflicting successors must continue to suppress authority while that trust state is retained; the implementation may choose how. Likewise, this node need not add a resolver, a global freshness oracle, automatic recovery after state loss, or session authentication. Those additions would exceed the accepted contract.

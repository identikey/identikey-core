## Blind take

1. Pin the device record to a credentials row; neither a contact channel nor a managed Sign key is a device.
2. Preserve list_devices as an XID-scoped list of non-revoked credential metadata, without trust or key-material claims.
3. Preserve the living promise that revoking an existing listed credential makes it disappear from the next list.
4. Pin revoke_device to soft revocation of the selected credential, with idempotence and XID isolation intact.
5. Refuse to infer a last-access check or confirmation mechanism from the current store API; neither exists there.
6. Require any account-level revocation policy to identify its enforcement boundary and preserve the store primitive's contract.
7. Require surviving access to mean usable account authentication, not merely an unrevoked row or a reachable mailbox.
8. Require any surviving-access decision and mutation to serialize together, respecting the existing custody-before-credential lock order.
9. Refuse documentation that presents planned account policy as behavior already enforced by this store.
10. Accept the tradeoff of an unconditional low-level revocation primitive if the higher-level policy has explicit ownership and atomic enforcement.

## Comparison and steelman

The ten-line take above was persisted before opening the delta, design, tasks, or previous review. Its product inputs were the proposal's Why, the living device-record requirement, and the cited store methods. Afterward I read the delta, design, tasks, and prior review in that order, then checked the full proposal for consistency.

The strongest objection to preserving an unconditional primitive is that it can remove the last login. The amended contract answers that objection by making last-access protection a policy of account-access revocation, rather than a guarantee of every credential mutation. That separation preserves existing callers and the living device scenario while giving account management one shared policy. The explicit requirement that later nodes call the guarded operation makes this a coherent boundary. It is not a claim that direct primitive calls prevent lockout.

The delta also answers the blind take's distinction between login access and other account material: it counts the named authentication kinds, excludes ssh_git, email, and the managed key, and preserves the separation between A0/A1 access and operational signing authority. My concern about usable access does not require a new provider-health probe or a different authentication model; the author has pinned the non-revoked credential kinds that qualify.

## Prior findings resolved

### 1. Enforcement and delivery boundary

Resolved. The amended delta at `specs/managed-custody/spec.md:60–90` explicitly preserves unconditional `revoke_device`, including a sole passkey, without a confirmation argument or surviving-access check. That matches the living "Revoke hides the device" scenario and `store.rs:680–706`, which soft-revokes the addressed row under the custody lock.

The separate account-access operation is kind-generic. The delta assigns both its implementation and verification to `identikey-core-zlf.6` for every counted kind, including a password row when that kind exists; later nodes must reuse it. This is ownership of enforcement, not merely ownership of a passkey confirmation screen. The proposal and `design.md:26–39` agree. The delta expressly says the rule becomes living behavior when that downstream operation lands and that folding this model does not constitute that landing. The model therefore makes no false claim about today's store.

### 2. Atomic surviving-access evaluation

Resolved. The delta at `specs/managed-custody/spec.md:75–84` and `design.md:31–38` require the decision and revocation in one account-scoped critical section, with custody locked before credentials. A count outside that critical section followed by the existing locked primitive would not satisfy this contract.

The concurrent scenario at `specs/managed-custody/spec.md:119–125` requires two unconfirmed revocations of two counted credentials to leave one active. It explicitly binds both decisions to the same account-scoped lock and lock order. Verification belongs to `identikey-core-zlf.6`. This closes the prior race without requiring runtime implementation on the model node.

## Blind pass

These ten lines were persisted in [2026-10-31-blind-take-2.md](2026-10-31-blind-take-2.md) before opening the delta, design, tasks, or previous review. Evidence was limited to the proposal's Why, the living managed-custody surviving-access requirement (`openspec/specs/managed-custody/spec.md:841–914`), and Papyrus `src-tauri/src/commands/identity.rs`.

1. Pin one named native client and list the clients deferred from the first account-enrollment journey.
2. Pin the distinction between creating a local device identity and binding its public key to an authenticated account XID.
3. Papyrus `device_enroll` only loads or creates a signer and returns public identity; it neither contacts the OP nor proves account ownership.
4. Its `device_authenticate` verifies a locally issued challenge, so refuse to treat that result alone as an OP login or enrollment proof.
5. Require an eventual account-binding contract to establish account authority and possession of the bound key without exporting its private material.
6. Keep `revoke_device` an unconditional soft-revoke even for the sole account credential; account-facing removal belongs to the separate account-access operation.
7. Reuse that operation's atomic, account-scoped surviving-access decision and custody-before-credential lock order rather than inventing native-specific policy.
8. Refuse to count a native credential row as surviving access until an implemented OP login can authenticate that account through it.
9. Distinguish hardware and software backends honestly; Papyrus can fall back to a persisted software seed, so enrollment cannot imply hardware assurance.
10. Tradeoff: a client-selection and credential-model decision may land before binding and login, provided its completion never claims those capabilities have shipped.

## Comparison and steelman

The strongest case for this narrow change is that choosing Papyrus and preserving future integration constraints is useful work on its own. My blind concerns about account authority, possession, and usable login do not require this decision node to design or implement the later protocol. The amended delta labels that later work explicitly, preserves device-signature consent on the signed-in XID, and leaves the hosted passkey journey independent. No new Elect key or protocol bead is introduced.

Papyrus's implementation supports the local-behavior claim: `identity.rs:139–141` returns the signer's public identity, while `device_authenticate` uses a local issuer and nonce store. The delta also explicitly permits fallback when hardware initialization fails (`specs/managed-custody/spec.md:13–16`), consistent with `device_signer`. The design's shorter backend description is therefore a preference description, not a hardware guarantee.

The separate `revoke_device` has no surviving-access confirmation branch. The amendment reuses the existing policy rather than creating another native policy.

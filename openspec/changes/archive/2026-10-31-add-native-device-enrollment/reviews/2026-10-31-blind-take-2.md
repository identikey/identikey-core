1. Pin one named native client and list the clients deferred from the first account-enrollment journey.
2. Pin the distinction between creating a local device identity and binding its public key to an authenticated account XID.
3. Papyrus `device_enroll` only loads or creates a signer and returns public identity; it neither contacts the OP nor proves account ownership.
4. Its `device_authenticate` verifies a locally issued challenge, so refuse to treat that result alone as an OP login or enrollment proof.
5. Require an eventual account-binding contract to establish account authority and possession of the bound key without exporting its private material.
6. Keep `revoke_device` an unconditional soft-revoke even for the sole account credential; account-facing removal belongs to the separate account-access operation.
7. Reuse that operation's atomic, account-scoped surviving-access decision and custody-before-credential lock order rather than inventing native-specific policy.
8. Refuse to count a native credential row as surviving access until an implemented OP login can authenticate that account through it.
9. Distinguish hardware and software backends honestly; Papyrus can fall back to a persisted software seed, so enrollment cannot imply hardware assurance.
10. Tradeoff: a client-selection and credential-model decision may land before binding and login, provided its completion never claims those capabilities have shipped.

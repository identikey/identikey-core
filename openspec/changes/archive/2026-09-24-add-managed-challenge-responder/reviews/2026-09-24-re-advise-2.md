## Independent take

Written before opening the change design, tasks, or managed-custody delta, from the packet Why, living specs, cited code, and LEARNINGS only.

1. Pin an Ed25519 operational Sign+Auth key whose stored private material demonstrably matches the current document public key.
2. Preserve the user's Elect authority, inception identity, and X25519 wrap recipient; refuse extending Schnorr into identikey-auth.
3. Accept only strict canonical protocol Challenge bytes and sign the protocol-defined transcript; refuse a general signing or private-blob API.
4. Pin challenge time bounds, audience and replay handling, with the edge retaining independent verification and consumption authority.
5. Require fresh A3 or user-held A4 authorization bound to the exact challenge digest and account; an account session alone cannot authorize C2 signing.
6. Bind authorization and response delivery to the initiating client so another caller cannot substitute a challenge, finish the ceremony, or retrieve its result.
7. Recheck current custody, credential validity, and key privileges at use, serializing against revocation, document updates, and C2 exit.
8. Commit authorization evidence before key use and completion or failure afterward; refuse response release when audit durability is uncertain.
9. Keep WebAuthn proof distinct from the returned protocol Response and the OIDC URN grant; explicitly define C1 policy and old-row refusal.
10. Accept reminting legacy Schnorr C2 identities as the compatibility tradeoff, while making loss of the old XID and unavailable user-private export explicit.

## Comparison and steelman

The current contract answers the independent take. Decisions 1 and 8 preserve the Elect/X25519 split while replacing only the operational signer, and require an authenticated seed envelope bound to the account and operational public. The signup/restart scenario verifies the persisted key against the document rather than a fresh test signer. Legacy bytes fail closed; reminting through signup means a new identity, not continuity of the old XID.

Decision 2 pins the actual v1 fields, exact-byte round-trip admission, limits, and expiry checks. I checked the local source at the Cargo-pinned identikey-protocol revision `fd073c0c5cb08f7102e73a4f2b7f9a529210cf98`: `Challenge` has no algorithm field, `Signer::respond` uses the protocol domain-separated payload, and `SoftwareSigner::from_ed25519_seed` exists. The edge's verification and nonce consumption remain outside this node.

Decision 5 now specifies an executable consent transcript: fixed-width account/request fields, a length-prefixed exact Challenge, the verifier commitment, and server-owned time, all inside the domain-tagged hash. I independently recomputed the vector and obtained `13ef62b41aa5240b246aec4d4bbb240fe5c94bb4a423c24898e2dee6a6844e35`. The `0102` input is a transcript-encoding fixture, not an admissible protocol Challenge. Exact server-returned A4 Challenge matching prevents replacement of its audience, nonce, or times. A3 can safely use the library's random challenge because its server-side association with the immutable attempt supplies the binding; rewriting WebAuthn's challenge is unnecessary.

The A4 authority rule is now concrete: a current Elect public, excluding the managed operational public, verified again before use. Decision 7 and the delta's explicit A4-without-passkey scenario answer the proof-specific authority concern. Hosted signup still cannot exercise A4 with its discarded Elect private; that limitation is stated rather than hidden behind an access-level label.

Decisions 6 and 7 make two defensible tradeoffs against a stricter reading of my take. First, an unknown post-claim outcome is terminal, sacrificing recovery to prevent a second key use; only a durable result and completion audit permit release. Second, the committed pre-decrypt recheck is the ordering point, so a request that wins may complete after revocation or exit. Holding locks throughout signing is not required to achieve this specified ordering. Both outcomes have acceptance scenarios. Existing document-update and exit paths lock the custody row, giving the implementation a concrete serialization seam; the required change to `revoke_device` is still implementation work.

The contract narrows delivery to C2 and retrieval to the original PKCE verifier. C1 support, offline delegation, local C3/C4 signing, Elect export, and edge token issuance remain outside this node. That bounded scope is acceptable despite the broader C1/C2 motivation.

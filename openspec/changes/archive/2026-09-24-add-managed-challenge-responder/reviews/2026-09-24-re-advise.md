## Blind take

Written before opening the change's design, tasks, or managed-custody delta; inputs were the packet's Why, the three living specs, cited signup/privilege/store code, and LEARNINGS lines 9–14.

1. Pin an Ed25519 managed operational key whose stored private material matches the current document's Sign+Auth public key; refuse implicit algorithm guessing.
2. Preserve the user's Elect/Transfer authority and X25519 wrap recipient; operational signing must never authorize document management.
3. Accept only canonical identikey-auth Challenge bytes and produce the protocol's Response; refuse arbitrary messages, unsupported versions, and Schnorr protocol extensions.
4. Bind each authorization to the exact challenge digest, account XID, audience, expiry, and initiating client, with explicit replay and concurrent-request behavior.
5. Require fresh A3 WebAuthn or independently verified user-held A4 proof for C2; an account cookie identifies a session but cannot instruct key use.
6. Verify proof against current account credentials or authorized user keys, refusing revoked credentials and the managed operational key as its own consent authority.
7. Make authorization durable before private-key use, record completed/failed outcomes, and define fail-closed behavior for audit failures and process crashes.
8. Serialize signing eligibility with custody/document changes so C3 exit, key removal, and authorization consumption cannot race into unauthorized key use or release.
9. Keep private material inside a narrow custody signer and return the Response solely to the initiating client; keep OIDC verification and edge custody policy separate.
10. Accept reminting legacy Schnorr C2 accounts as the bounded compatibility tradeoff, but make the refusal actionable and do not imply signup already preserves user-held Elect secrets.

## Comparison and steelman

The contract answers most of the take. Decisions 1–2 preserve Elect/X25519, choose Ed25519 without changing v1, enforce exact Challenge roundtripping, and bound admission. Decisions 5–6 bind the client commitment and account to an immutable attempt, introduce an atomic claim, make unknown key use terminal, and commit the Response with its completion audit. Decision 8 names an authenticated seed envelope and a real signup→restart→load verification case. These are substantive resolutions; the earlier crash ambiguity is closed.

I accept the design's tradeoffs over stricter readings of my take: C2-only delivery, PKCE-only retrieval, a server-side WebAuthn instruction binding, and a signing winner that may complete after exit/revocation. At-most-once key use can sacrifice recovery after a crash. Core need not authenticate arbitrary edge audiences or perform edge nonce consumption. Exact SQL layouts and handler names can remain implementation choices.

Two remaining gaps affect the supported A4 path. They need short contract amendments, not another architecture. This send-back does not reopen the accepted envelope, crash, or A3 race decisions.

## Required revisions

### R9 — Make the consent transcript reproducible

**Evidence:** `design.md:67–71,79–88`; delta `specs/managed-custody/spec.md:42–47`.

The text lists fields and calls their bytes canonical, but does not choose an encoding or show how `identikey-managed-consent-v1` enters the hashed bytes. XID representation, request-id representation, framing, and verifier-commitment representation remain unspecified. Two clients can follow this field list and produce different SHA-256 digests. The separate A4 Challenge also lacks a defined mapping for its `iat` and `exp` and an explicit exact-match rule against the server's expected consent Challenge.

The pinned protocol's `Signer::respond` signs the full canonical Challenge, including both timestamps; its generic `verify_response` validates an audience/time window and consumes a nonce, but does not know this managed-consent transcript. Naming those APIs therefore does not fill the gap.

Pin one unambiguous, domain-tagged encoding with field types, the commitment algorithm/representation, server-owned consent time, and all five consent-Challenge fields. State whether begin returns the exact bytes to sign and require finish to verify the expected consent Challenge for the immutable attempt. A compact wire example is sufficient; no new general signing protocol is needed.

**Acceptance:** one fixed transcript→digest→consent-Challenge vector reproduced by client and server; a valid Elect-signed A4 Response reaches retrieval with the original verifier; account, request, commitment, or consent-Challenge substitution fails before decrypt.

### R10 — Define the A4 branch of the pre-use authority check

**Evidence:** `design.md:79–88,122–132`; delta `specs/managed-custody/spec.md:42–55`.

A4 authority is an Elect key in the current XID document. The mandatory pre-decrypt check nevertheless requires a credential-row lock for every attempt. Neither begin nor A4 enrollment identifies such a row, and document membership does not imply a `credentials` record. An implementation must currently invent an A4 credential mapping, reject an otherwise eligible Elect proof, or silently waive a SHALL.

Qualify the checks by proof kind. For A3, retain the specified account credential and custody locks. For A4, identify the authority record to lock and explicitly recheck that the exact consenting public still holds Elect, is not the managed key, and belongs to this account at the same pre-use ordering point. If the current document is the sole authority, say so; a second credential registry is unnecessary. Preserve the declared winning-signature retrieval policy.

**Acceptance:** a supported protocol-compatible Elect key with no passkey row can complete A4; removal of that key or its Elect privilege before the recheck prevents decryption; a recheck that wins follows the documented completion policy. Also retain the owed operational Sign+Auth removal race case, which is not currently an explicit delta scenario.

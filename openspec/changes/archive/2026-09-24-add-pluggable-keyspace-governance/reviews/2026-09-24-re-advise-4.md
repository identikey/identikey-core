## Independent blind take

Written before opening the current proposal's design, tasks, or governance delta; inputs are the packet's Why, living guild-keyspaces spec, cited implementation, ADR-012, and cited learnings.

1. Pin the existing v1 genesis-hash identity, roster-admin authorization, and replica authority until an explicit upstream versioned transition is available.
2. Pin authorization, finality, and secret release as separately evidenced decisions, with compatibility rules that reject unsupported combinations before mutation or disclosure.
3. Require signed bootstrap authority and outgoing-policy-authorized handoff bound to exact predecessor, successor, scope, and replay domain; refuse residual founder or operator privilege.
4. Require deterministic handling of concurrent authority changes and finality reversals, freezing at a verifiable last agreed state without inventing an administrative tie-breaker.
5. Make recovery an optional authority declared before failure, with explicit scope and activation conditions; refuse recovery that silently becomes a universal override.
6. Bind the collective's stable XID to the keyspace's authenticated history without replacing the genesis-hash keyspace ID or silently rewriting ADR-012 for v1.
7. Separate reading membership, collective Sign/Auth, identity-control powers, and delegated agency; require independent authorization for each and bounded, revocable delegation.
8. Identify each Registry trust domain independently of Lightning discovery and make migration preserve provenance, identity continuity, and the governing verification rules.
9. Preserve member-XID Elect boundaries, user-held encapsulation targets, and the ban on locally invented protocol kinds or key algorithms; secret release cannot revoke already learned plaintext.
10. Accept the availability cost of explicit freeze and predeclared recovery in exchange for eliminating hidden authority, provided adversarial cases and upstream prerequisites make the choice testable.

## Comparison and steelman

The architecture answers most of the blind take at the appropriate proposal depth.
The version cut leaves the v1 implementation and member-XID boundaries intact;
the independent collective inception and dual-authorized binding preserve both
identifiers without a circular hash. Identity control is enforced at wallets,
resolvers, and relying parties rather than being a core-only policy promise.
Reader membership, Sign/Auth, identity control, and bounded agency are separated.
Whole-secret custody is distinguished from threshold cooperation, without choosing
a cryptographic library or inventing a core wire extension.

The handoff phases keep staged documents and inactive keys from becoming current.
They explicitly preserve predecessor authority while pending and admit irreversible
disclosure. Settlement migration uses outgoing finality and separately labeled
incoming readiness. Registry identity and upgrade authority remain independent of
service discovery. These answer blind concerns 1–3 and 5–9.

Consume records now give new actions an explicit ordering boundary against handoff
(design.md:137–148; delta:366–382). Completion of a previously consumed action is
distinct from a delayed first use or replay. Durable witnesses and stated fault
assumptions acknowledge the availability price in concern 10. Concrete wire
encoding, quorum implementation, and consume execution/retry mechanics can remain
upstream/downstream work; this review does not ask for runtime implementation.

The strongest reading of the revised conflict model is also sound: approval alone
does not select authority; one predecessor-authorized settlement record selects a
successor, and contradictory settlement records freeze. A withheld approval cannot
retroactively become settlement. The remaining blocker is that the earlier binding
requirements still prescribe a different result for this very case.

## R12 — Reconcile the remaining approval-fork freeze rule (blocking)

The delta's **Conflict and settlement semantics** still requires conflicting
approved off-chain changes to freeze at the predecessor, and **Delivery order
differs** requires both replicas to report that conflict (spec.md:73–85).
design.md:234–238 likewise gives off-chain forks an unqualified freeze rule.
But **Finality gates secret release** requires an approved sibling rejected by
settlement to leave the recorded successor effective (spec.md:245–255 and
269–279; design.md:262–271).

Concrete counterexample: predecessor P names an off-chain witness set. Owners
approve A and B; the witness records A, A activates, and the witness refuses B.
A replica receiving B after A must freeze under spec.md:75–85, but must keep A
effective under spec.md:245–249 and 269–273. Both approval delivery orders expose
the contradiction. One consumer can stop identity actions and releases while
another continues under the same profile and evidence. This is a governance
availability and interoperability defect, not a request to change the chosen
settlement architecture.

I considered reading the earlier rule as applying only to approval-only profiles.
That exception is not stated. The design also calls `local-quorum` and `registry`
the two admitted settlement classes (design.md:240–252), then assigns settlement
to a witness set or Registry head while saying owner signatures are only approval
(design.md:254–260). Thus the text does not supply a clear profile boundary that
makes the two requirements disjoint. Later placement alone does not supersede an
earlier SHALL or its acceptance scenario.

Required revision: scope or replace the earlier freeze requirement and scenario,
and reconcile the settlement-class terminology with the admitted witness-set
profile. State which, if any, approval-only profile remains admissible and what
can become effective without a settlement record. The same profile and evidence
must yield one result throughout the design and delta. Preserve the current
distinction between a refused approval sibling and settlement equivocation.
Exercise zero, one, and two settlement records with approvals A/B delivered in
both orders, including recovery versus handoff. This is one bounded reconciliation
of the current contract, not a new mechanism or a request to implement it.

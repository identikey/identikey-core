## Independent blind take

Written from the packet Why, living guild-keyspaces spec, cited code, ADR-012, and related learnings before opening the proposed design, tasks, or delta.

1. Pin the unchanged v1 roster-admin rule, genesis hash identifier, seven op kinds, and explicit version boundary for every new authority mechanism.
2. Pin separate meanings for authorization, settlement/finality, and secret release; agreement on one must not silently imply the others.
3. Require a compatibility contract that names the assumptions and failure behavior of every permitted mechanism combination.
4. Require signed bootstrap and a handoff authorized by the outgoing policy, binding the exact destination policy and history without residual founder or operator power.
5. Pin conflict detection and the last agreed state, including concurrent handoffs, delayed evidence, and the authority that can resolve a freeze without circular authorization.
6. Permit recovery only through previously authorized rules with bounded scope; refuse a support, founder, or deployment-admin recovery backdoor.
7. Bind a stable collective XID to the existing keyspace history while distinguishing reader access, collective Sign/Auth, identity-control powers, and delegated agency.
8. Pin Registry instance identity independently of Lightning discovery so endpoint replacement cannot replace the community authority or defeat replay isolation.
9. Refuse local wire inventions, implicit Elect on membership changes, Sign+Auth as a wrap target, and claims that policy revocation retracts already disclosed secrets.
10. Accept reduced availability under conflict or unavailable finality as a tradeoff for authority continuity, provided adversarial scenarios and upstream protocol gates make that cost explicit.

## Steelman against the blind take

- **Version and authority boundary (take 1, 9).** `design.md:31-62` and delta “Version cut rejects legacy administering” preserve the v1 baseline and require an upstream migration cut. The text explicitly accounts for an unaware old replica accepting stale v1-shaped operations; governance-aware consumers must reject that authority. It does not pretend to retrofit old clients. The collective XID is an actor identifier bound to the existing genesis-hash history, not a replacement keyspace identifier. No local wire additions, dependencies, or wrap-target changes are authorized.
- **Mechanism composition (take 2, 3).** `design.md:10-29,279-305` separates approval, settlement, and release, with recognized verifiers, pinned versions and domains, mutual compatibility declarations, bounded evidence, and fail-closed results. Contract evidence is not confused with a group signature; operator custody is not presented as threshold cooperation. Concrete mechanisms still owe upstream encodings and executable verification, appropriately outside this proposal.
- **Bootstrap, handoff, recovery (take 4, 6).** The signed-bootstrap requirement and `design.md:169-232` bind replacement authority to the outgoing policy and incoming usable-control evidence. Staged documents and inactive successor keys do not become current. Missing effects or settlement leave the last activated policy in force; abort cannot recall disclosed secrets or restore an implicit founder privilege. Settlement migration uses outgoing finality and separately labeled incoming readiness. Recovery must already be authorized by the effective predecessor policy.
- **Conflicts and finality (take 5).** `design.md:234-277`, delta “Conflict and settlement semantics,” and “Finality gates secret release” now agree on zero, one, and two settlement records. Owner approvals alone select neither branch. One settled successor survives revelation of an unrecorded approved sibling; two contradictory settlement records freeze at their predecessor. Recovery races obey the same rule. A later checkpoint cannot invent a resolution authority. Settlement selection remains subject to the explicit activation prerequisites in delta “Handoff phases are independently abortable” and “Pre-activation effects are not current”; a receipt does not waive missing effects or activate a staged document.
- **Identity and freshness (take 7).** `design.md:64-167` establishes a noncircular, jointly authorized XID/keyspace binding and separates readers, Sign/Auth, document control, and bounded delegation. Enforcement is required across wallets, resolvers, and relying parties, with no inception-key fallback. Matching historical bundles are insufficient. New action authority is ordered by an action-bound consume record against handoff, rather than by a cached observation's age. The delta covers stale bundles, restart, partition before consume, delayed first use, earlier committed work completing later, and replay at another consumer.
- **Registry trust and availability (take 8, 10).** `design.md:112-148,279-309` pins authority separately from discovery, declares operator/consensus trust and witness fault assumptions, and requires durable ordering. Moving an endpoint does not change authority; changing the settlement domain does. The cost is explicit: new collective actions need an admissible witness and consume, and may be unavailable during partition. Existing plaintext remains usable. Late equivocation cannot undo an earlier disclosure, but stops further release.

The strongest reason to accept is that the proposal makes these enforcement obligations common to all admitted mechanisms. It neither assumes signatures produce finality nor assumes a self-consistent old identity bundle proves current authority. The remaining wire formats, concrete quorum/consensus parameters, cryptographic suites, recovery procedures, and runtime evidence are downstream work already identified by the contract, not grounds to substitute a competing architecture in this review.

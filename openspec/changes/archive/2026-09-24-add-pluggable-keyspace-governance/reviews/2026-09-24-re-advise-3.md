Disposition: `task-red`. Two contract issues below prevent acceptance of the current text. This is an independent other-family review of the files on disk; earlier review bodies were not consulted.

Independent take, written before opening the current design, tasks, or governance delta, using the supplied Why, living guild-keyspaces spec, cited implementation, ADR-012, and recorded key facts:

1. Pin an explicit version boundary: existing v1 roster-admin authorization, actor restrictions, genesis identity, and epoch behavior remain unchanged until an upstream-governed transition.
2. Pin independent authorization, finality, and secret-release contracts, including which combinations are valid and what evidence each consumes.
3. Pin signed bootstrap and handoff to a precise prior state, outgoing authority, incoming policy, and activation boundary; refuse any retained founder or operator override.
4. Pin recovery before failure, with its authority, trigger, and limits committed in advance; refuse emergency authority invented after lockout.
5. Pin conflicting transitions to the last agreed state with explicit rules for settlement, recovery, and continued activity; refuse arrival-order winners and silent merges.
6. Pin a stable guild XID's authenticated relation to the genesis-hash keyspace history, including migration and uniqueness, without silently rewriting ADR-012's v1 identity contract.
7. Pin separate reader membership, collective Sign/Auth, identity-control permissions, and delegated agency; refuse a PTK, membership, or actor signature as an implicit grant of all four.
8. Pin Registry instance identity, authority, and finality evidence independently of Lightning discovery endpoints, including endpoint substitution and stale or foreign proofs.
9. Pin protocol-owned encodings and adversarial acceptance evidence before runtime dispatch; preserve user encapsulation targets and refuse local op kinds, local fields, or a fabricated X25519 roster algorithm.
10. Accept the availability cost of freeze and unavailable mechanisms to preserve authority and identity, while stating what old ciphertext access and already released secrets cannot be revoked.

**Comparison and steelman.** The proposal answers most of that take. The version cut preserves v1 semantics and acknowledges stale legacy replicas. XID-first construction avoids a circular identifier hash; dual authorization and collective-aware verification address retained inception keys. The handoff separates inactive successor keys and staged documents from current authority, and settlement migration uses outgoing finality with separately labeled incoming readiness. Registry pins, upgrade authority, evidence bounds, explicit compatibility, predeclared recovery, and the distinction between custody and threshold cooperation are appropriate architecture commitments. Deferring concrete suites, wire encoding, and runtime is consistent with this node. I am not requesting those implementations here.

The freshness section also makes a substantial, useful commitment: a signed old bundle is insufficient, witnesses have declared trust assumptions, and unseen owner signatures do not require omniscience. My remaining objections concern the observable decisions those rules require, rather than the choice of mechanism.

**R10 — Blocking: define the ordering point between action use and handoff.** References: `design.md:112-143`; `specs/keyspace-governance/spec.md:312-358`.

The Registry rule admits an action-bound head read with a maximum age. The ordering paragraph rejects an action if a newer observation reports movement, then promises that every outstanding witness becomes void as soon as the head moves. Those are different guarantees. A verifier can read head P for action X, the handoff can activate H, and the verifier can use the still-within-age witness for X without receiving another observation. All parties can be honest. Binding X prevents substitution; it does not establish whether H preceded consumption of X. A delayed first use of that exact witness has the same problem as replay. Re-reading immediately before use merely moves this gap unless use and handoff have a defined common ordering point.

Strongest reading: “at use time” and “wins the witness first” intend an ordered authorization/consumption operation. That would be a sound architecture direction, but the current contract specifies an observation as input and does not say when the action is irrevocably authorized, how that event is ordered against activation, or whether later physical completion counts as a new use. The explicit scenario requires rejection after movement even for an already issued witness, so an implementer cannot safely assume issuance is that ordering point.

Owed contract edit: define that ordering boundary and the evidence/participation required to enforce it for both witness types. State what happens to delayed completion and to a verifier partitioned after its last successful observation. If the intended guarantee is bounded staleness instead, state that limit and reconcile the unconditional revocation scenarios; maximum age alone cannot provide immediate revocation. No concrete consensus algorithm or wire field is requested.

Acceptance cases: (a) read/attest P for X, activate H, then first-use X while its witness is within age and the consumer has learned nothing further; (b) the reverse ordering, with X committed before H but completed afterward; (c) replay the same action at the same and a second consumer; (d) loss of witness connectivity between observation and use. Each needs one specified authorization result and an explicit boundary for irreversible release.

**R11 — Blocking: reconcile authorized forks with witness-selected finality.** References: `design.md:119-130,229-255`; `specs/keyspace-governance/spec.md:73-85,243-267,312-352`.

Under `local-quorum`, complete outgoing-owner signatures are called final; two conflicting approved off-chain successors require a freeze. The witness section instead says a branch the quorum never recorded is not final and is rejected if the quorum already recorded A. Consider fully owner-approved siblings A and B: the witness records A, a release occurs for A, and B is later delivered to both the verifier and that witness. The witness refuses B without equivocating. The general approved-fork/finality rules require freezing A's dependent actions; the new witness rule rejects B without saying that A freezes. This affects authority and future release, not just terminology.

Strongest reading: the design intends to distinguish a final authorization certificate from a final effective successor, allowing the predecessor's witness mechanism to settle which approved branch is effective. That is a defensible separation of authorization and settlement, but it must be explicit in the admitted mechanism combinations and in the earlier unconditional freeze requirements. A freshness witness must not silently become an additional settlement authority with different conflict semantics.

Owed contract edit: distinguish approval, settlement finality, activation, and action freshness, and state which conflicting evidence triggers freeze for each admitted combination. Reconcile the general approved-fork rule and the handoff/recovery race scenario with the witnessed-branch exception. If witness recording selects the successor, identify that power as predecessor-authorized ordering and state its interaction with `local-quorum` finality. If B instead triggers freeze when learned despite witness rejection, say so explicitly and distinguish that from the period when B was withheld.

Acceptance cases: fully signed A recorded by the witness with fully signed B withheld; B then revealed and refused; two siblings actually recorded through witness equivocation; and a fully authorized recovery racing a handoff under the same profile. Replicas receiving the same evidence in different orders must reach the same specified state.

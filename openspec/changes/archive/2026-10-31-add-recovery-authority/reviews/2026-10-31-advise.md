## Blind pass

The following ten lines were persisted in `reviews/2026-10-31-blind-take.md` before opening the delta, design, or tasks. Inputs were only proposal Why and the three requested living requirements; the packet cited no implementation code.

1. Pin recovery authority to restoring account access; an A0 or A1 proof must never directly exercise the managed Sign key.
2. Pin the recovered passkey to the ordinary signup credential model, with identical listing, login, consent, and revocation behavior.
3. Require an explicit boundary between a recovery proof and subsequent ordinary passkey authentication before operational signing authority is available.
4. Bind every recovery authorization to one account, one purpose, a bounded lifetime, and one successful credential-registration ceremony.
5. Refuse replay, cross-account substitution, and concurrent double redemption; proof consumption and credential creation must have a defined atomic outcome.
6. Keep verified email on accounts.email under its existing case-insensitive uniqueness rule; recovery must not synthesize an email credential or device.
7. Refuse any recovery path that silently decrypts, signs with, rotates, or replaces managed_key_blob, or mints Elect from A0/A1 proof alone.
8. Preserve existing access unless an explicitly specified operation revokes it; recovery must not invent a second surviving-access policy or alter revoke_device.
9. Require failed, expired, or conflicting recovery attempts to leave account access and operational key material unchanged, with testable negative cases.
10. Accept the tradeoff that mailbox recovery can establish a new ordinary login, provided its proof cannot be reused as an operational signing credential.

## Finding requiring amendment

**R1 — Preserve the full authority ceiling in the normative delta.** `design.md:9-11` says the A1 proof and recovery session do not read `managed_key_blob` and do not grant Elect or Transfer. `specs/managed-custody/spec.md:12-15` instead prohibits decrypting the blob, signing a Challenge, and minting an Elect key. It says nothing about granting Transfer, and minting a key is narrower than granting Elect authority to an existing key. Reading the encrypted blob also remains outside the delta's prohibition. The three scenarios do not close these differences.

A recovery session that attached privileged authority to a caller's existing key could obey the delta's literal prohibitions while violating the design's boundary. This is a specification gap, not a claim that such a runtime path exists. The living requirement at `openspec/specs/managed-custody/spec.md:804-823` repeats the narrower decrypt/sign/mint restrictions; it does not supply the missing explicit grant prohibition.

Amend the delta to carry the design's no-read and no-Elect/Transfer-grant restrictions for both A1 proof and recovery session. Add a negative scenario that preserves that boundary after the ordinary passkey has been enrolled: the recovery session itself still cannot exercise those authorities. Keep the fresh passkey assertion on ordinary consent terms. This amendment belongs to this node's authority matrix; no ceremony implementation is requested.

## Comparison and steelman

The core decision is sound. The delta at lines 5-15 answers blind concerns 1-3 and 10: enrollment produces one ordinary `passkey`, introduces neither a purpose flag nor an upgrade assertion, and requires a fresh assertion for subsequent C2 signing. The living consent requirement at `openspec/specs/managed-custody/spec.md:243-280` already binds that assertion to a specific consent attempt and rejects a long-lived session as signing authority. Recovery therefore does not need a parallel consent protocol.

The strongest objection to my blind take is scope. This node records an authority matrix; it does not implement mailbox verification or registration transactions. Requiring all replay, expiry, concurrency, and rollback mechanics here would expand the node unnecessarily. Blind concerns 4, 5, and 9 remain implementation acceptance concerns for the eventual recovery ceremony, not additional blockers on this matrix. The adjacent `add-email-magic-link` delta covers single-use, account-bound address verification and explicitly inserts no credential; it should not be mistaken for a completed recovery enrollment ceremony. This review makes no claim that those later mechanics are already covered or implemented.

Blind concerns 6 and 8 are compatible with the change as written: the new row is a passkey, not an email credential, and the delta neither changes `accounts.email` nor invents revocation policy. The living account-channel and surviving-access requirements continue to apply. An ordinary recovered passkey consequently participates in the same counted-access model as a signup passkey.

The best argument for accepting despite R1 is that the design already states the intended restriction and an implementer could follow it. That is insufficient for this particular node: its deliverable is the authority contract that will be folded into living requirements. The delta needs to preserve the decided ceiling without relying on the design to restore omitted permissions. No competing passkey model or extra upgrade step is needed.

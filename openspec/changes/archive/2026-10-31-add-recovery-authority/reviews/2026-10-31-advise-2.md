## Blind pass

Inputs were the proposal's Why and living managed-custody requirements for email, the managed Sign key, surviving access, and ordinary C2 consent. The packet cited no implementation code.

1. Pin A1 recovery to the account's verified email channel; the address itself must never become a credential or surviving access.
2. Pin successful recovery to an ordinary passkey on that same account, with the usual listing, revocation, and access-counting semantics.
3. Refuse a recovery-only passkey class that permanently weakens or complicates ordinary account consent.
4. Refuse any managed-key read, decrypt, or signing authority conferred by the A1 proof or the recovery session.
5. Refuse Elect or Transfer authority conferred by recovery, including grants to existing keys; banning Elect-key minting alone is insufficient.
6. Pin that authority ceiling across enrollment: adding a passkey must not upgrade the still-live recovery session into signing or management consent.
7. Permit a fresh assertion of the enrolled passkey to satisfy ordinary C2 A3 consent, bound to the exact Challenge and request with current credential and custody rechecks.
8. Refuse resurrection of discarded Elect material or substitution of the mailbox, account cookie, or managed Sign key for user-held A4 authority.
9. Keep surviving-access policy in the designated account-access revocation operation; recovery enrollment must not redefine the unconditional revoke primitive.
10. Accept the tradeoff that mailbox recovery can establish a future ordinary A3 authenticator, while requiring a distinct fresh assertion before managed signing.

## R1 disposition

**Resolved.** `specs/managed-custody/spec.md:12-19` now explicitly applies the no-read, no-decrypt, no-sign, no-Elect/Transfer-grant, and no-Elect-mint restrictions to both the A1 proof and the recovery session. The grant prohibition expressly includes an existing key. This closes the prior gap: an implementation cannot comply merely by avoiding key generation while assigning management authority to a caller's existing public key.

The same paragraph retains all those limits on the recovery session after enrollment. The negative scenario at lines 42-47 asks that session to read the blob or grant Elect/Transfer after creating an ordinary passkey and requires refusal. Although that scenario does not repeat every prohibited operation or specify the target key's age, the normative paragraph explicitly covers both; there is no remaining permission exception. Before-enrollment signing refusal is separately covered at lines 35-40.

Lines 17-19 and 28-33 preserve the positive path: a fresh assertion of the enrolled passkey uses the same C2 signing-consent terms as a signup passkey. Enrollment and a reused recovery session cannot stand in for that assertion. The living consent requirement at `openspec/specs/managed-custody/spec.md:243-280` supplies the exact Challenge/request binding, expiry, current credential/custody rechecks, and rejection of long-lived session authority. The amendment does not create an alternate signing ceremony.

## Comparison and steelman

The ordinary `passkey` row and explicit absence of a purpose flag, second class, or upgrade assertion answer blind concerns 2 and 3. The new ceiling and its persistence across enrollment answer concerns 4-6. Ordinary fresh C2 consent answers concerns 7 and 10. The unchanged living email, discarded-Elect, and surviving-access requirements answer concerns 1, 8, and 9: email stays an account channel, recovery cannot reconstruct discarded Elect, and enrollment does not redefine either revocation operation or what counts as access.

The strongest objection to accepting is that mailbox control can eventually lead to managed signing by enrolling an ordinary passkey. That is the explicit recovery tradeoff in Why, not a contradiction in the authority ceiling. The ceiling constrains the A1 proof and recovery session; it does not permanently taint the new credential. Requiring a permanent restriction or an extra upgrade ceremony would defeat the chosen ordinary-passkey model. A distinct, fresh ordinary A3 consent assertion is the required boundary.

Conversely, the blind take's account binding must not turn this matrix review into a demand for a full recovery protocol. Token expiry, consumption, enrollment atomicity, and the loss-of-all-passkeys drill remain later ceremony and verification work. This review does not claim those mechanics have landed. The present node needs to state the authority boundary unambiguously, and the amended delta does so without relying on the living text's narrower decrypt/sign/mint prohibition at `openspec/specs/managed-custody/spec.md:804-823`.

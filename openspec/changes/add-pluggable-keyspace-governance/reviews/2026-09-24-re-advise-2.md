## Independent take (written before reading the proposed contract)

1. Pin the v1 roster-admin contract and genesis-hash keyspace identity until a separately versioned upstream protocol explicitly replaces their rules.
2. Pin the guild XID as a distinct collective identity with an authenticated, durable binding to keyspace history, without re-identifying the keyspace or electing its members.
3. Require each governance mechanism to state who authorizes, how a decision becomes final, and who can release secrets; none of those powers implies the others.
4. Require explicit compatibility rules between those mechanisms, including proof verification, finality assumptions, and the result of missing or contradictory evidence.
5. Require signed bootstrap and predecessor-bound handoff authorized under the outgoing policy, with a defined activation boundary and replay resistance.
6. Refuse residual founder, host, registry operator, or service-provider authority after handoff; recovery must have been explicitly authorized beforehand.
7. Pin concurrent transitions to the last agreed state and define which actions freeze, how resolution is authorized, and what happens to irreversible disclosures.
8. Keep reader membership, collective Sign/Auth, XID-control privileges, and delegated agency separately scoped and revocable; PTK possession is not governance authority.
9. Pin Registry instance identity independently of discovery endpoints, with authenticated history across migration and no acceptance of an endpoint as proof of authority.
10. Tradeoff: prefer explicit refusal and temporary loss of availability over ambiguous authority; require adversarial acceptance cases and upstream wire work before runtime dispatch.

## Comparison and steelman

The current contract answers most of this take. The version cut preserves v1 as
the baseline and puts new wire work in identikey-protocol. The dedicated XID and
two-sided binding avoid a circular identifier construction. Collective control
is enforced beyond core, with separate membership, agency, custody, and document
powers. Outgoing-policy authorization, explicit recovery, and conflict freeze
exclude an implicit founder override. Recognized verifiers, evidence bounds,
compatibility declarations, and Registry pins keep discovery outside authority.

The handoff text now makes a useful distinction between an inactive replacement
key and disclosure of an existing secret. Staged documents do not confer current
control, and activation requires the completed effects. Registry migration also
has a clear authority boundary: outgoing finality, separately labeled incoming
readiness, then incoming settlement for later transitions. These resolve the
corresponding concerns in my take at architecture scope. Concrete encodings,
cryptographic libraries, and runtime repairs can remain downstream.

The strongest reading of the freshness addition is that a profile must supply a
trusted online authority and otherwise deny every new action. That is a sound
direction. However, the text does not state the trust and ordering conditions
that make this authority's answers sufficient, and one acceptance case promises
more than either defined witness establishes. This is the remaining blocker.

## R9 — Make freshness and partition guarantees implementable

**High priority; contract correction before acceptance.** References:
`design.md:106–120`, `design.md:214–230`, and
`specs/keyspace-governance/spec.md:312–340`.

The design defines `registry-head` as a finalized head and `witness-set` as an
attestation of its latest checkpoint and lack of a known sibling. The delta
requires both partitioned readers to refuse whenever two signed siblings exist,
even when each sees only one. These definitions leave two gaps:

1. **A valid witness can itself be stale.** Before handoff, an attacker captures
   the document, binding, checkpoint, and a genuine head/witness attestation.
   After handoff, it presents the entire bundle to a restored or isolated
   verifier. The witness is present and names the matching checkpoint, satisfying
   the stated witness checks. A finalized checkpoint proves finality at that
   point; its bytes alone do not prove it is still the head. The contract needs
   a verification boundary for a current observation, including replay/rollback
   resistance and the permitted race between observing authority and using it.
   Binding evidence to the transition does not distinguish two uses of the same
   old transition at different times.
2. **No known sibling is not proof that no sibling exists.** Under the admitted
   `local-quorum` definition, owners can sign A and B from predecessor P while
   withholding B from the witnesses. Even a live, honest witness can report A as
   its latest state and know no conflict. Reader A then has all the described
   evidence, while the partition scenario requires refusal because B exists.
   There is no threshold, intersection/fault assumption, durable non-equivocation
   rule, or required serialization of authorization/release through witnesses
   that excludes this execution. Merely requiring more fresh responses cannot
   reveal a deliberately hidden signed branch.

**Owed correction:** define the architectural obligations of an admissible
freshness mechanism. State its trusted parties/fault assumptions, how evidence
is made current for the consuming action, how restart and replay are handled,
and where concurrent handoff and use are ordered. For a witness set, state the
quorum/non-equivocation and durable-state obligations if those are relied upon;
for a Registry, distinguish proof of a finalized checkpoint from a trusted current
head observation. Profiles that cannot meet the chosen guarantees must be
inadmissible for these actions, rather than passing merely by naming a witness.
This does not require a library, wire format, or consensus implementation here.

Reconcile the partition scenario with the chosen model. Either require an
explicit serialization mechanism that prevents incompatible successors from
becoming actionable (and distinguish a signed branch from a settled one), or
state the bounded freshness and detected-conflict guarantees actually offered.
Do not claim universal detection of an unseen signed sibling. Preserve the
existing honest statement that a later-discovered fork cannot recall released
plaintext.

Add acceptance cases for a replayed *complete* witness bundle after handoff, a
live witness unaware of a withheld fully signed sibling, and a handoff concurrent
with an outstanding action witness. Give each a definite outcome under the
declared assumptions. The existing missing-witness case does not cover them.

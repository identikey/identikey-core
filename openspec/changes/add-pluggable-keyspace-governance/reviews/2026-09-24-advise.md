# Architecture review: add-pluggable-keyspace-governance

The direction is sound, but the common verification contract is not yet defined
enough to accept. The draft explicitly leaves its security-critical decisions to
future specification. This verdict requires contract preparation, not runtime work.
Disposition: `task-red`.

## Independent blind take

Written to [2026-09-24-blind-take.md](2026-09-24-blind-take.md) before opening
the proposal design, tasks, or governance delta. Inputs were the packet's Why,
living guild-keyspaces spec, cited code/ADR, and supplied learnings. Reproduced unchanged:

1. Pin an explicit version boundary: existing v1 logs retain roster-admin authorization and their current genesis-hash identity.
2. Pin separate authorization, finality, and secret-release contracts; require evidence that a chosen combination enforces the same accepted state.
3. Pin signed bootstrap and outgoing-policy authorization of handoff, bound to the precise predecessor, successor policy, keyspace, and replay domain.
4. Refuse implicit founder, operator, resolver, or recovery authority after transfer; any recovery must be predeclared and narrowly authorized.
5. Pin deterministic handling of competing transfers and settlement reversals at the last agreed state, including the limits of freezing already released secrets.
6. Pin the collective XID's authenticated binding to keyspace history and its continuity across controller changes without inventing a replacement keyspace identifier.
7. Pin distinct reader membership, collective Sign/Auth, XID control, and delegated agency powers; membership and shared-secret possession must not imply control.
8. Pin Registry instance identity and trust roots independently of Lightning endpoints, including migration, stale views, and equivocation.
9. Refuse core-local wire inventions, premature runtime dispatch, and changes to C2 wrap-target or actor restrictions; protocol-owned contracts and adversarial cases must precede implementation.
10. Tradeoff: prefer a visible loss of governance availability when authority or finality conflicts over an undocumented override, while stating what existing reads and signatures can still do.

## Comparison and steelman

The author answers the direction of all ten concerns. Particularly strong choices
are outgoing-policy approval of the whole replacement, distinct-principal counting,
explicit operator custody versus threshold custody, and Registry identity independent
of discovery. Keeping the keyspace genesis ID while adding an actor XID is a coherent
successor to ADR-012; it does not require replacing the keyspace identifier. The
non-circular binding candidate is reasonable. The draft also correctly acknowledges
that a freeze cannot retract plaintext and that a core-only policy check cannot
secure an otherwise unrestricted XID key.

The strongest case for acceptance is that this is architecture preparation and
correctly delegates wire ownership and backend selection. I agree that it need not
select a FROST library, build consensus, or implement any verifier. However,
`design.md:114-119` makes wire reconciliation, identity enforcement, atomic
activation, and finality this node's deliverables. Naming these seams and leaving
them open does not establish the common contract that downstream adapters must obey.

## Blocking findings

### R1 — Specify the version and migration rejection boundary

`design.md:5-7,37-47` promises old-client safety and immutable IDs but gives no
upstream transition contract. Today's `keyspace.rs:213-232,464-477` dispatches seven
kinds and authorizes using a roster boolean. An unaware v1 replica can still accept
a v1-shaped operation from an actor it considers admin; a new consumer's refusal of
unknown mechanisms does not fix that case.

Provide protocol-owned references or an explicit upstream contract proposal covering
new genesis, existing-keyspace migration, legacy continuation, and how consumers
recognize the authoritative version. State the guarantee for clients that never
learn a migration. Reconcile admin, fade, outer/inner nesting, and epochs. Require
cases for stripped governance evidence, old-admin continuation after migration,
and governed nesting. Do not solve this by inventing local fields or kinds.

### R2 — Close collective identity binding and verifier enforcement

`design.md:37-43,58-63` leaves both binding proof and control enforcement open.
`privilege.rs:96-111` authorizes Elect from a key in the previous document; it does
not evaluate collective governance. A binding certificate alone therefore cannot
establish that every identity-control path obeys a quorum policy.

Specify the authenticated history and current-state evidence required by wallets,
resolvers, and relying parties, including unsupported/stale-verifier behavior and
how conflicting bindings of one XID to two keyspaces are rejected. Map Sign/Auth,
Elect/Transfer/Update, and delegation to that enforcement boundary. Acceptance
cases must include a retained inception/controller key, a stale valid document,
and simultaneous competing bindings. Preserve both IDs and member-XID isolation.

### R3 — Define handoff as a recoverable state transition

`design.md:71-79` says partial execution is pending while explicitly deferring
resume/abort. If an identity rotation has taken effect but settlement fails, the
outgoing state may already be unusable; calling the transfer pending does not say
who may act or whether abort can restore anything.

Specify preparation, activation, failure, resume, and abort semantics, the evidence
at each boundary, and which policy authorizes each action. Cover settlement-backend
migration as well as owner replacement. Require interruption/retry cases at each
external effect, stale approvals, delegation retirement, and inability to undo key
disclosure. The contract may restrict supported combinations where safe activation
cannot be established; it must not assume a transaction spanning independent systems.

### R4 — Define finality before irreversible effects and competing recovery

`design.md:86-92` and delta `spec.md:73-90` handle an observed fork but do not define
when a single observed successor becomes effective. A replica may release a new
secret on branch A before learning branch B. Eventual agreement to freeze cannot
undo that release. Registry finality, reorg, and rollback behavior are also unnamed
contracts rather than specified verifier outcomes (`design.md:101-106`).

Define provisional/final evidence and freshness obligations for each admitted
settlement class, including the stated assumptions that make release permissible.
Specify authority and ordering when normal handoff, recovery, or fork resolution
compete under the same predecessor. Add late-fork-after-release, partitioned/stale
reader, recovery-versus-handoff, and finalized-checkpoint contradiction cases with
explicit outcomes. Freeze and availability limits are acceptable; hidden fallback
or claims of retracting already exposed secrets are not.

### R5 — Make compatibility and Registry trust checkable

`design.md:12-29,96-106` requires compatible mechanisms and pinned Registry identity
without defining compatibility predicates or the evidence needed to evaluate them.
Thus independently valid authorization, settlement, and release evidence could
refer to different effective transitions without a specified composition check.

Define a minimum common adapter contract, evidence/resource bounds, and admitted
versus rejected combinations. Bind all evidence to the same transition and pinned
verification context. Specify Registry code/upgrade-authority changes and their
relationship to outgoing-policy authorization, including same-address code changes.
Add mixed-state/domain evidence, oversized evidence, unauthorized upgrade, and
Registry migration cases. Concrete backend implementations can remain downstream.
